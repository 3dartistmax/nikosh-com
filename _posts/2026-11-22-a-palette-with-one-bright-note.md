---
title: "A Palette with One Bright Note"
date: 2026-11-22
excerpt: "A limited palette can make one accent color do more expressive work."
---

Color can feel more deliberate when most of the palette stays quiet. Choose a few neighboring or neutral tones, then give one color a brighter role. That accent can guide the eye toward a focal shape or create contrast in an otherwise calm scene.

The key is proportion and repetition. If the bright note appears everywhere, it stops being a note; if it appears once without support, it may feel detached. Small echoes in a shadow, a line, or a detail can connect it to the rest.

Try limiting a sketch to three supporting colors and one accent. The constraint makes relationships easier to see—and leaves room for surprises.
